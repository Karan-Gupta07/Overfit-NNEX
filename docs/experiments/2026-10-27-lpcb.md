# Image-local probability coding on LPCB, 2026-10-27

## Outcome and scope

On the 107 images of the Lossless Photo Compression Benchmark (LPCB), the
photo coder came out 1.21% smaller in total than paq8px v216 -9L, the
benchmark's top entry: 877,080,059 bytes against 887,823,381. That is also
3.28% smaller than cmix v16 and 9.76% smaller than GraLIC 1.11, the next two
entries. Every image is one output file, coded with the same settings, and
every file decoded exactly.

The win comes from the 62 camera photos: 3.91% smaller than paq8px in total,
and smaller on every one of them (by 0.81% to 5.36%). On the 20 star fields
the two are level (0.08% smaller in total, ours smaller on 16). On the other
25 NASA images (renders, composites, processed views of planets and
spacecraft) ours is 22.7% larger in total, and paq8px wins 22 of them.

Against the standard lossless formats the photos came out 16.24% smaller
than JPEG XL effort 10, the best of them on every photo, and smaller than it
on all 62 (details below).

No setting was chosen on LPCB. The images were first measured at `299bc4c`;
the run reported here is `2db19a3`, which only adds the one-file strip format
for the five largest images (below). The other 102 files came out
byte-identical to the first run. This is not an official LPCB entry.

## Against PNG, WebP and JPEG XL

Measured here on the whole images, the camera photos came out 16.24% smaller
than JPEG XL effort 10 in total (2.15 against 2.57 bits per sample), 24.97%
smaller than WebP lossless and 37.51% smaller than PNG. JPEG XL effort 10 was
the best of the four baselines on every photo, and ours was smaller than it
on all 62, by 12.5% to 20.7% (median 15.8%). Over all 107 images ours is
15.21% smaller than the per-image best baseline and smaller on 99. The eight
it does not beat are non-star NASA images: six by 1.5% to 6.0%, PIA13915 by
337 bytes (its file is the JPEG XL floor in our container) and PIA13779 by
55%.

The baselines use the same settings as every other result in this
repository: PNG from Pillow with `optimize`, `cwebp 1.6.0 -lossless -z 9

1,194,936,668 and 1,432,319,902 bytes, within 0.3% and 1.5% of ours. LPCB
lists no JPEG XL result.

## Data

LPCB ([main page](http://qlic.altervista.org/LPCB.html), Alex Rhatushnyak)
ranks lossless codecs on 107 24-bit RGB PPM images, 3,462,571,880 bytes or
1,154,190,026 pixels: 62 photographs converted from camera RAW samples (27
Olympus XZ1, 12 Sony A55, 12 Fujifilm X100, 11 Canon 1100D, 10 to 16 million
pixels each) and 45 NASA Photojournal images (25 of planets, spacecraft and
artwork, 20 of stars, galaxies and nebulae; 0.2 to 46 million pixels).

The benchmark's own PPM archive (`lpcb.ppm.zip`, 2,361,154,127 bytes) is gone
from its original host, but the Wayback Machine keeps the 2014 copy.
`fetch_lpcb.py` downloads it with the benchmark's MD5 list and its per-image
paq8px, cmix and GraLIC results, and refuses to continue unless every image
matches its published MD5 sum. All 107 matched, so these are the pixels the
published sizes measured, and the per-image sizes add up to the leaderboard
totals. The coder reads the PPM files directly.

LPCB counts one output file per input file. A single network takes at most
16,777,216 pixels, and five images are larger: PIA13757 (18.2 million
pixels), PIA13912 (37.2 million) and STA13843, STA13844 and STA13845 (46.2
million each). Their files hold two or three equal horizontal strips, each a
complete coded file with its own model, behind a small table of strip
heights and lengths (31 bytes for two strips, 39 for three). The first rows
of a strip cannot see the strip above it. The five together came out 4.8%
larger than paq8px (162.2 against 154.7 MB). Most of that is PIA13912
(+21%); STA13845, at +7.19%, is the only star field paq8px won by more than
1%.

## Protocol

`lpcb_validation.py` ran `compress.compress` on each PPM, one image at a
time, then `compress.decompress` on the saved file, and compared the decoded
samples with the source before keeping the size. The five strip images ran
first, then the other 102. `lpcb_baselines.py` had measured the four
baselines on the same whole images, six at a time (9.8 CPU-hours, most of it
JPEG XL effort 10).

The laptop (Apple M4 Pro, 12 CPU cores, 16-core GPU, 24 GB) stayed on AC
power, logged every 20 s from 26 minutes in. A check just before that showed
battery power once, during canon_eos_1100d_13 (62.4 s, against 51 to 55 s for
the other Canon photos). Encoded again alone afterwards, on AC, that photo
gave a byte-identical file in 66.0 s, so the switch did not slow it. A chat
app and a browser were open in the background. Encoding all 107 took 1.64
hours and decoding 0.23 hours. The training processes peaked at 9.7 GB.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|

| Sony A55 | 12 | 16.2 million | 58.8 s (54.2 to 68.1) | 11.67 s (11.28 to 12.14) |

A photo's encode is mostly training on the GPU (median 37 s), while the CPU
builds the JPEG XL floor at the same time. It also includes the least-squares
fits (about 5 s) and a full decode that checks the file (about 9 s).
Decoding runs the network on eight CPU threads.
Raw receipts: `docs/results/raw/lpcb-2db19a3.json` and
`lpcb-2db19a3-retime.json` (not in git; the summary keeps every number).

The published paq8px, cmix and GraLIC sizes are from their testers' runs on
other machines. paq8px v216 -9L, tested by Zoltan Gotthardt on an Intel
i7-1165G7, took 891,585 s (248 hours) to compress the 107 images and 901,650
s to decompress them, single-threaded: a median of 2.56 hours to compress
each camera photo and 2.58 hours to decompress it. cmix took about nine
times longer.

**Checking the files.** `verify_lpcb.py` decodes every `<name>.coded` file,
rebuilds the PPM bytes (`P6\n<width> <height>\n255\n` and the RGB samples, the
header all 107 images use), and compares their MD5 with LPCB's
`md5sums.txt`. Run on the saved files after the encode run, it found all 107
matching (877,080,059 bytes) in 14 minutes of decoding. From a fresh install
with only the base packages and `imagecodecs` (no PyTorch), and a decoder
built from the committed source, it also matched four of them: one plain
file, one all-floor strip file, one mixed strip file and one small floor
file.

**Second architecture.** `verify_cross_isa.py` checked ten of the files with
an x86_64 build of the decoder under Rosetta, on the same Mac: two photos per
camera, one star field and one other NASA image. Each decoded to the same
pixels as the ARM64 build, and re-encoding its stored model with the x86_64
build gave the same bytes. This is not a second physical machine. Receipt:
`docs/results/raw/lpcb-cross-isa-2db19a3.json`.

**Earlier run.** At `299bc4c` the five strip images were coded as separate
files per strip, summed (187 bytes smaller in total), and to finish sooner
everything after the fifth image ran two encodes at once, so per-image times
from that run (63 to 119 s per photo) measure throughput, not speed. Its
summary is [`2026-10-27-lpcb-299bc4c.json`](../results/2026-10-27-lpcb-299bc4c.json).

## Where it loses

The 25 non-star NASA images vary most: from 7.6% smaller than paq8px
(PIA13872) to 3.1 times paq8px's size (PIA13779, a 5.4-million-pixel image
paq8px codes in 49 kB). Ten of the 28 coded parts (counting strips) kept
the `scdx` floor (JPEG XL or WebP) because the network did not beat it.
Five images make up 12.2 MB of the 14.1 MB gap: PIA13912 (+21%, 4.7 MB),
PIA13915 (+44%, 3.9 MB), PIA13810 (+37%, 1.6 MB), PIA13894 (+36%, 1.4 MB)
and PIA13882 (+31%, 0.6 MB). These are processed or composited images where
long-range matching and paq8px's many image and general models help. GraLIC
does no better: on the 25 together it is 22.2% larger than paq8px, against
our 22.7%. The photo coder only sees local neighbourhoods and two local
least-squares fits, so it has no way to reuse a pattern from far away.

## Reproduction

```bash
LP=scripts/research/local_probability
python $LP/fetch_lpcb.py
git checkout 2db19a3   # the measured commit
python $LP/lpcb_validation.py --out docs/results/raw/lpcb-2db19a3.json \
  --files data/lpcb-coded $(ls data/corpora/lpcb/ppm)
python $LP/lpcb_baselines.py --out docs/results/raw/lpcb-baselines.json
(cd $LP && python summarize_lpcb.py --baselines ../../../docs/results/raw/lpcb-baselines.json \
  --out ../../../docs/results/2026-10-28-lpcb-2db19a3.json ../../../docs/results/raw/lpcb-2db19a3.json)
(cd $LP && python verify_lpcb.py ../../../data/lpcb-coded)
```

The run above listed the five strip images first. To check the released
files without encoding, download and unpack the release, build the decoder
(see the [README](../../README.md#run-it)), and run `verify_lpcb.py` on the
unpacked directory.

## Limits

- Not an official LPCB result: the codec was not submitted, and the
  published sizes come from other people's runs of other programs.
- Five images were coded as strips, which only made our total larger.
- Timings are from one Apple M4 Pro laptop; paq8px's are from another
  machine, so compare them only as orders of magnitude.
- The learned coder is research code: 8-bit RGB only, a GPU to encode at
  this speed, and decoding several times slower than JPEG XL.
