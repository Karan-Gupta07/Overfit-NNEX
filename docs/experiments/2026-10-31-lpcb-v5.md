# NNEX v5 on LPCB, 2026-10-31

## Outcome and scope

NNEX v5 codes the per-image network's predictions again through a
context-mixing model. At commit `ea118a7` it coded the 107 images of the
Lossless Photo Compression Benchmark (LPCB) in 854,572,981 bytes. That is
3.75% smaller in total than paq8px v216 -9L, the benchmark's top entry
(887,823,381 bytes), 5.76% smaller than cmix v16 and 12.08% smaller than
GraLIC 1.11. Ours is smaller than paq8px on 90 of the 107 images and smaller
than cmix on 100. Every file decoded exactly, and all 107 match LPCB's MD5
sums.

**This is not a fresh result.** v5 was developed with LPCB in the loop: its
contexts, its mixer rates and the rules that decide when and how a file
mixes were chosen while measuring LPCB images, mostly the NASA ones. The
fresh LPCB measurement is still the v4 coder at `2db19a3`, 1.21% smaller
than paq8px ([notebook](2026-10-27-lpcb.md)). On the fresh Tecnick photos,
where no v5 setting was chosen, v5 came out 0.33% (16 crops) and 0.04% (four
2400×2400 images) smaller than v4 and 1.39% and 1.79% smaller than paq8px,
but took 2.4 and 1.6 times as long to encode
([re-measure](2026-10-26-fresh-tecnick.md#re-measure-with-nnex-v5-at-ea118a7)).

## What v5 changes

The network, its training and its weight coding are as in v4. v5 changes
how the samples are coded:

- **Context mixing.** The smallest network file is coded again with the
  network's probability of every bit as one input to gated mixers, beside
  hashed contexts of neighbouring samples, bit histories, the runs of equal
  samples that end at the sample from the left and from above, and the
  network's recent errors. A second mixer layer, adaptive probability maps
  and a learned blend follow. R and B are coded as differences from G. The
  context model learns as it codes, so the decoder repeats it. The mixed
  file is kept only if it saves at least 0.002 bytes per pixel, because it
  decodes several times slower.
- **Bands.** Planes can split into column bands, each with its own context
  model, so they decode on more threads at a small cost in bytes. Images up
  to 1 Mpx try one band, ungated and gated; up to 2 Mpx, one band and two.
  Larger images mix in one band (two over 8 Mpx), plus one more if mixing
  saved under 10% of the network's bytes on a screened band of rows.
- **Value sets.** Images whose planes share a gapped set of sample values
  code ranks in that set, and a plane that leaves at least eight values
  unused skips the bits those values decide.
- **Grey planes.** R and B planes that nearly repeat G also try a network cut
  to a constant.
- **Strips** of the five largest images may share one model.
- **Widths** race on their estimated mixed size, and every width that
  finishes training mixes.

Mixing was kept on 4 of the 62 photos, 20 of the 25 other NASA images and
8 of the 20 star fields (a strip file counts if any of its strips mixed).
The four photos are fujifilm_finepix_x100_13 and _15, olympus_xz1_16 and
sony_a55_11, which came out 0.42% to 2.48% smaller than at `2db19a3`. The
other 58 photos kept the network alone and came out 10,907 bytes smaller in
total (from 32,382 bytes smaller to 8,412 larger per photo). No coded part
kept the SCDX floor, against ten parts at `2db19a3`.

## Protocol and speed

`lpcb_validation.py` ran `ea118a7` with a clean tracked tree, one image at a
time, the other NASA images first, then the star fields, then the photos.
Each encode includes checking its file: a mixed candidate is decoded as it
is coded, on a canvas of its own, and a network-only file is decoded once
it is written. `compress.decompress` then decoded every saved file and the
samples were compared with the source.

The laptop (Apple M4 Pro, 12 CPU cores, 16-core GPU, 24 GB) was on AC power,
with a browser and several chat apps open; up to about 4 GB of swap was in
use. Encoding all 107 took 1.89 hours (1.64 at `2db19a3`) and decoding 0.25
hours (0.23).

| Sony A55 | 12 | 63.6 s (56.5 to 105.9) | 8.92 s (7.98 to 28.42) | 58.8 s, 11.67 s |
| Fujifilm X100 | 12 | 52.7 s (49.9 to 75.1) | 6.34 s (6.17 to 16.88) | 54.5 s, 9.33 s |

| **all 62** | | **52.5 s** (47.0 to 105.9) | **6.20 s** (4.94 to 28.42) | 54.1 s, 9.06 s |

A photo's median encode spends 37.7 s training on the GPU while the CPU
builds the JPEG XL floor, 2.8 s on the least-squares fits, 5.4 s on the
weight search, 1.6 s screening mixing and 6.2 s checking the file. Photos
that kept the network alone decoded about a third faster than at
`2db19a3`, from `7eb6a2c`, which runs the same integer arithmetic with less
work per sample. The four mixed photos decoded 1.8 to 2.3 times slower than
at `2db19a3` (sony_a55_11 in 28.4 s against 12.1 s).

The NASA images pay most of the time. The other 25 took 1,597 s to encode
(1,036 s at `2db19a3`) and 191 s to decode (53 s); the star fields 1,680 s
(1,493 s) and 274 s (216 s). The slowest decodes are the strip files
STA13844 (65 s), STA13843 (64 s) and PIA13912 (51 s, 19.8 s at `2db19a3`).
The largest process peaked at 12.4 GB.

Raw receipt: `docs/results/raw/lpcb-ea118a7.json` (not in git; the summary
keeps every number).

## Where it still loses

paq8px is still smaller on 17 of the 25 other NASA images, by 0.07%
(PIA12813) to 17.8% (PIA13779). On those 17 the gap totals 217,472 bytes,
and our stored weights account for 139,090 of them: these files are small,
or flat enough that paq8px codes them in little more than the weights take.
The worst are PIA13779 (+17.8%; 3.1 times paq8px's size at `2db19a3`),
PIA13799 (+8.7%, was +60.7%), PIA13862 (+5.7%, was +11.6%), PIA13815
(+4.9%, was +40.2%) and PIA13857 (+4.7%, was +10.0%). The best are PIA13872
(−7.5%) and PIA13914 (−6.5%).

The stored weights are 0.18% of the photo bytes, 0.20% of the star-field
bytes and 0.43% of the other NASA bytes.

## Checks

**Checking the files.** `verify_lpcb.py` decoded every saved file, rebuilt
its PPM and found all 107 matching LPCB's `md5sums.txt` (854,572,981 bytes)
in 15.5 minutes of decoding. Receipt:
`docs/results/raw/lpcb-verify-ea118a7.json`.

**Second architecture.** `verify_cross_isa.py` checked 16 of the files with
an x86_64 build of the decoder under Rosetta, on the same Mac: two photos per
camera (for Fujifilm, Olympus and Sony one of the two mixed in three bands),
PIA12811 and PIA12813 (grey planes cut to a constant, mixed and not),
PIA13799 (mixed in one ungated band), PIA13803 (two gated bands), PIA13810
(one gated band), PIA13833 (network alone), STA13458 (network alone, B cut
to a constant) and STA13771 (three bands). Each decoded to the same pixels
as the ARM64 build, and re-encoding its stored model with the x86_64 build
gave the same bytes. The checker takes single files only, so the five strip
files were not in it. This is not a second physical machine. Receipt:
`docs/results/raw/lpcb-cross-isa-ea118a7.json`.

## Limits

- Not fresh: LPCB images were used to tune v5 (above). Not an official LPCB
  result either.
- Mixed files decode several times slower than the network alone: 2.5 to
  3.2 times the median for the four mixed photos (three bands per plane),
  and about five times for one-band images such as PIA13810 (16.8 s against
  3.3 s for its `2db19a3` file). The largest need several GB of memory to
  decode.
- Timings are from one laptop with other apps open; paq8px's published
  times are from another machine.
- The learned coder is research code: 8-bit RGB only, and a GPU to encode at
  this speed.
