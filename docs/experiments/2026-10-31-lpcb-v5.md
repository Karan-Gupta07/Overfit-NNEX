# NNEX v5 on LPCB, 2026-10-31

## Outcome and scope

NNEX v5 codes the per-image network's predictions again through a
context-mixing model. At commit `de30ed3` it coded the 107 images of the
Lossless Photo Compression Benchmark (LPCB) in 854,571,285 bytes, and
`2063b53` re-encoded the three images it changes to the same files. That is
3.75% smaller in total than paq8px v216 -9L, the benchmark's top entry
(887,823,381 bytes), 5.76% smaller than cmix v16 and 12.08% smaller than
GraLIC 1.11. Ours is smaller than paq8px on 90 of the 107 images and smaller
than cmix on 100. Every file decoded exactly, and all 107 match LPCB's MD5
sums.

**This is not a fresh result.** v5 was developed with LPCB in the loop: its
contexts, its mixer rates and the rules that decide when and how a file
mixes were chosen while measuring LPCB images, mostly the NASA ones. The
fresh LPCB measurement is still the v4 coder at `2db19a3`, 1.21% smaller
than paq8px ([notebook](2026-10-27-lpcb.md)). On the fresh Tecnick photos,
where no v5 setting was chosen, v5 at `2063b53` codes the 16 crops 0.26%
smaller than v4 (`da035fe`) and the four 2400×2400 images 150 bytes
smaller, 1.32% and 1.75% smaller than paq8px, at about 1.25 times v4's
median encode time
([re-measure](2026-10-26-fresh-tecnick.md#late-width-race-and-grey-screen-at-de30ed3-and-2063b53)).
The first v5 run there, at `ea118a7`, had taken 2.4 and 1.6 times as long.

## What v5 changes

The network, its training and its weight coding are as in v4. v5 changes
how the samples are coded:

- **Context mixing.** The smallest network file is coded again with the
  network's probability of every bit as one input to gated mixers, beside
  hashed contexts of neighbouring samples, bit histories, the runs of equal
  samples that end at the sample from the left and from above, and the
  network's recent errors. A second mixer layer, adaptive probability maps
  and a learned blend follow. R and B are coded as differences from G. The
  context model learns as it codes, so the decoder repeats it. The mixed
  file is kept only if it saves at least 0.002 bytes per pixel, because it
  decodes several times slower.
- **Bands.** Planes can split into column bands, each with its own context
  model, so they decode on more threads at a small cost in bytes. Images up
  to 1 Mpx try one band, ungated and gated; up to 2 Mpx, one band and two.
  Larger images mix in one band (two over 8 Mpx), plus one more if mixing
  saved under 10% of the network's bytes on a screened band of rows.
- **Value sets.** Images whose planes share a gapped set of sample values
  code ranks in that set, and a plane that leaves at least eight values
  unused skips the bits those values decide.
- **Grey planes.** R and B planes that nearly repeat G also try a network cut
  to a constant.
- **Strips** of the five largest images may share one model.
- **Widths** race on their estimated mixed size, those left meet again
  after an eighth of training, and every width that finishes training
  mixes.

Mixing was kept on 4 of the 62 photos, 20 of the 25 other NASA images and
8 of the 20 star fields (a strip file counts if any of its strips mixed).
The four photos are fujifilm_finepix_x100_13 and _15, olympus_xz1_16 and
sony_a55_11, which came out 0.42% to 2.48% smaller than at `2db19a3`. The
other 58 photos kept the network alone and came out 10,907 bytes smaller in
total (from 32,382 bytes smaller to 8,412 larger per photo). No coded part
kept the SCDX floor, against ten parts at `2db19a3`.

## Protocol and speed

`lpcb_validation.py` ran `de30ed3` with a clean tracked tree, one image at a
time, the other NASA images first, then the star fields, then the photos.
Each encode includes checking its file: a mixed candidate is decoded as it
is coded, on a canvas of its own, and a network-only file is decoded once
it is written. `compress.decompress` then decoded every saved file and the
samples were compared with the source. The three images that `2063b53`
changes were then re-encoded at `2063b53` the same way
([grey constants](#grey-constants-at-2063b53)).

The laptop (Apple M4 Pro, 12 CPU cores, 16-core GPU, 24 GB) was on AC power,
with a browser and several chat apps open; up to about 4.5 GB of swap was in
use. Encoding all 107 took 1.68 hours (1.89 at `ea118a7`, 1.64 at
`2db19a3`) and decoding 0.24 hours (0.25 and 0.23).

The photos took 3,085 s in total to encode against 3,392 s at `2db19a3`, and
423 s to decode against 557 s. A photo's median encode spends 33.8 s
training on the GPU while the CPU builds the JPEG XL floor (37.7 s at
`ea118a7`), 2.6 s on the least-squares fits, 4.9 s on the weight search,
1.5 s screening mixing and 6.0 s checking the file. Photos that kept the
network alone decoded about a third faster than at `2db19a3`, from
`7eb6a2c`, which runs the same integer arithmetic with less work per
sample. The four mixed photos decoded 1.7 to 2.3 times slower than at
`2db19a3` (sony_a55_11 in 27.9 s against 12.1 s).

The NASA images pay most of the time. The other 25 took 1,387 s to encode
(1,597 s at `ea118a7`, 1,036 s at `2db19a3`) and 185 s to decode (191 s and
53 s); the star fields 1,567 s (1,680 s and 1,493 s) and 268 s (274 s and
216 s). The slowest decodes are the strip files STA13843 (62.9 s), STA13844
(62.6 s) and PIA13912 (50.0 s, 19.8 s at `2db19a3`). The largest process
peaked at 11.8 GB (12.4 GB at `ea118a7`).

## Width race at `10fbde6`

Images up to 4 Mpx, and larger ones whose residuals are compressible, train
two network widths side by side and race them after a 32nd of training. At
`ea118a7` a width kept training if its estimate was within 3% of the best,
plain or with mixing counted, or while the weights of the best mixed
estimate were at least a tenth of it. That last rule kept both widths on all
23 LPCB images that raced without dropping one, and on all 12 development
Tecnick crops, which doubled their training time.

`10fbde6` applies the weights rule only to widths narrower than the leader.
That is the PIA13779 case: width 48 led at the race and width 24 finished
12% smaller once mixed. A wider width has more weights still, so it keeps
training only within the 3% margins. Replayed on the receipts of 36 images
(12 development Tecnick crops, the development native fence and these 23),
no width that the new rule drops won; every wider winner had been within
3% (cushions at 2.9%). On LPCB only PIA13855, PIA13856, PIA13859 and PIA13862
now drop the wider width, which none of them kept. They re-encoded to
identical files in 24 to 40 s instead of 42 to 64 s, so the `ea118a7`
results held for `10fbde6`. The development crops also re-encoded to identical
files, in a median 11.1 s instead of 22.2 s. Measured afterwards on the
fresh Tecnick crops, the rule halved their encode time and cost one crop,
guitar_head, 1,095 bytes: its width 32 had trailed by 3.3% at the race
([re-measure](2026-10-26-fresh-tecnick.md#width-race-at-10fbde6)).

## Late width race at `de30ed3`

Over about 6.5 Mpx a 32nd of training is the first epoch, and scores taken
that early ranked the widths almost at random: on PIA13943 width 24 trailed
by 6.9% at the race, led after the second epoch and finished smaller.
`de30ed3` races after at least two epochs. With mixing on, the widths left
meet again after an eighth of training, and a width stops there if a wider
one scores lower, unless the wider model's weights are at least 5% of its
mixed estimate. Traced on 12 development crops, 12 development 2400×2400
images trained at both widths and 26 LPCB images, no width this stops would
have won. The same commit keeps each worker's base features as int16
values, which they are, instead of float64 (3 GB for three planes at
2400×2400); two workers side by side had pushed macOS into compressing
memory. The predictions sum exactly either way.

The whole of LPCB was re-encoded at `de30ed3`. Every file is identical to
`ea118a7`'s except PIA13943, which kept width 24 and came out 1,696 bytes
smaller (paq8px's lead on it fell from 2.34% to 1.81%). All 107 encoded in
6,098 s instead of 6,793 s and decoded in 876 s instead of 908 s; the photos
alone in 3,085 s instead of 3,515 s, with identical files. On the fresh
Tecnick photos, measured afterwards, the late race stopped width 24 on the
native ducks, where width 48 scored 0.14% lower and its weights were 1% of
its estimate. Width 24 would have finished 2,500 bytes (0.16%) smaller. The
four native encodes took a median 50.8 s instead of 63.8 s
([re-measure](2026-10-26-fresh-tecnick.md#late-width-race-and-grey-screen-at-de30ed3-and-2063b53)).

## Grey constants at `2063b53`

A grey image whose best file was the network alone also mixed its
constant-plane file in full, which no LPCB image ever kept. Over 2,000,000
pixels `2063b53` first codes that file's screened band and mixes it only if
mixing saves 0.001 bytes per pixel there, as it does for the network. The
three LPCB images this applies to, PIA13757 (two strips), PIA13943 and
STA13458, re-encoded to identical files in 211 s instead of
270 s. The tables above use those three runs.

## Where it still loses

paq8px is still smaller on 17 of the 25 other NASA images, by 0.07%
(PIA12813) to 17.8% (PIA13779). On those 17 the gap totals 215,776 bytes,
and our stored weights account for 136,516 of them: these files are small,
or flat enough that paq8px codes them in little more than the weights take.
The worst are PIA13779 (+17.8%; 3.1 times paq8px's size at `2db19a3`),
PIA13799 (+8.7%, was +60.7%), PIA13862 (+5.7%, was +11.6%), PIA13815
(+4.9%, was +40.2%) and PIA13857 (+4.7%, was +10.0%). The best are PIA13872
(−7.5%) and PIA13914 (−6.5%).

The stored weights are 0.18% of the photo bytes, 0.20% of the star-field
bytes and 0.43% of the other NASA bytes.

## Checks

**Checking the files.** `verify_lpcb.py` decoded every saved file, rebuilt
its PPM and found all 107 matching LPCB's `md5sums.txt` (854,571,285 bytes)
in 15.3 minutes of decoding. Receipt:
`docs/results/raw/lpcb-verify-2063b53.json`.

**Second architecture.** `verify_cross_isa.py` checked 17 of the files with
an x86_64 build of the decoder under Rosetta, on the same Mac: two photos per
camera (for Fujifilm, Olympus and Sony one of the two mixed in three bands),
PIA12811 and PIA12813 (grey planes cut to a constant, mixed and not),
PIA13799 (mixed in one ungated band), PIA13803 (two gated bands), PIA13810
(one gated band), PIA13833 (network alone), PIA13943 (network alone at
width 24, two planes cut to a constant), STA13458 (network alone, B cut to
a constant) and STA13771 (three bands). PIA13943 and STA13458 are the
`2063b53` files. Each decoded to the same pixels
as the ARM64 build, and re-encoding its stored model with the x86_64 build
gave the same bytes. The checker takes single files only, so the five strip
files were not in it. This is not a second physical machine. Receipt:
`docs/results/raw/lpcb-cross-isa-2063b53.json`.

## Limits

- Not fresh: LPCB images were used to tune v5 (above). Not an official LPCB
  result either.
- Mixed files decode several times slower than the network alone: 2.5 to
  3.4 times their camera's median for the four mixed photos (three bands per
  plane), and about five times for one-band images such as PIA13810 (15.9 s
  against 3.3 s for its `2db19a3` file). The largest need several GB of memory to
  decode.
- Timings are from one laptop with other apps open; paq8px's published
  times are from another machine.
- The learned coder is research code: 8-bit RGB only, and a GPU to encode at
  this speed.
